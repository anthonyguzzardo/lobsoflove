# Wilson racket paint jobs: current and previous generation

Research date: 2026-10-23. Scope: the six Wilson lines in `web/lib/gear-catalog.js` (BLADE 98, BLADE 100, PRO STAFF 97, CLASH 100, ULTRA 100, SHIFT 99), plus RF 01 / RF 97.

Method: generation and release dates come from Wilson press releases, wilson.com product pages (Shopify product JSON), and news coverage. Colors were read from Tennis Warehouse product photos. Hex values were eyeballed and then checked against pixel samples from those photos. Studio lighting and metallic paint shift the readings, so treat every hex as approximate (about +/- 10%).

Photos live in `docs/research/rackets/photos/wilson/`. File types:
- `*_front.jpg` is Tennis Warehouse's main image. It shows the side profile on the left and the full string-bed front on the right, so it covers both views.
- `*_throat.jpg` is a close-up of the throat and wordmark.
- `*_angle.jpg` and `*_hoop.jpg` show the head from an angle or close up.
- `*_grip-buttcap.jpg` shows the handle and the butt cap.

Every file was opened and checked by eye.

Note on string stencils: Wilson rackets are sold unstrung. The red W drawn on the strings in every Tennis Warehouse photo is the retailer's own graphic overlay, not part of the paint. On court, the Wilson W stencil is applied by the stringer. It is usually black ink on white or natural strings, and often white or red on dark or colored strings. The W is the blocky, flat-topped "W" mark seen in the photos, centered in the string bed.

## BLADE 98 and BLADE 100

The Blade 98 and Blade 100 share one cosmetic in each generation. Only the head size and string pattern differ.

### Current: Blade v10 (2026)

- Launched April 15, 2026. Wilson calls it the 10th edition of the Blade. The line has 8 models (98 16x19, 98 18x20, Pro 98 16x19 and 18x20, Pro 100, 100, 100L, 100UL, 104).
- Main frame: "Championship Green" (Wilson's name), a gloss metallic emerald green with a strong highlight sheen. Approximate hex `#17804f`. Highlights reach `#2fa56d` and the shaded side drops to `#0f5e3a`.
- Colorshift: there is no true chameleon shift, only a metallic flake that brightens toward lighter green in highlights.
- Top of the head (about 10 to 2 o'clock): the bumper guard is a slightly darker, flatter green (about `#1f6a47`). It is not black, so the head reads as one green from a distance.
- Sides (3 and 9 o'clock): a single short infrared (red-orange) tick at about 4 o'clock on the face. Wilson calls this an "infrared hot spot". Approximate hex `#ff4a2a`.
- Throat and shaft: the same metallic green. The inner bridge is green.
- Wordmark: a large "Wilson" in pale mint green (`#8fe3b8`), running up the outer face of one shaft. A small infrared "W" mark sits by the wordmark. "B L A D E" is spaced out in mint capitals down the other shaft.
- Finish: gloss metallic over the frame, with a satin bumper.
- Grommets: green. A mint-green bumper clip sits at 10 o'clock.
- Handle: green grip (`#2f6e4f`) with a tonal Wilson logo.
- Butt cap: red with a white Wilson W, set in a green surround. Small infrared rings sit on the handle collar.
- Head shape: oval, the classic Blade shape. The v10 adds 1 mm of beam height in the upper hoop, which gives a slight taper. Open throat, flat-sided box-ish beam.
- Common special editions:
  - US Open 2026 Blade 98 16x19 / Blade 100 v10: a cream / off-white frame (`#ece8e0`) with a navy upper hoop band, a royal blue "Wilson" wordmark, and a white grip.
  - Fall Layers (October 2026): a navy frame with a sage / taupe upper hoop.
  - Both editions are recent, and the US Open cream one is the most likely to show up on courts.

## PRO STAFF 97

### Current: Pro Staff 97 Classic (January 2026)

- Wilson sells it as the "Pro Staff 97 Classic", not as "v15". Tennisnerd and some retailers call it v15, and reviewers describe it as a repaint of the v14 with the same specs. As of October 2026, it is the only standard (non-limited) Pro Staff 97 sold on wilson.com.
- Main frame: black, approximate hex `#1e1f21`. Wilson's listing says "Black!". A Decathlon listing calls it "matte black". A customer review mentions a gloss upper hoop. Photos show a satin black with a slightly glossier upper hoop.
- Colorshift: none.
- Top of the head: black, with no separate band color.
- Sides (3 and 9 o'clock): a thin horizontal stripe on each side made of a red line (`#d6202a`) over a yellow line (`#f2c21b`). It echoes the 1980s Pro Staff Original and "Classic".
- Throat and shaft: black. The same red and yellow stripe wraps the shaft just above the grip.
- Wordmark: a large "Wilson" in silver-grey (`#b9bbbd`) on the outer shaft. "PRO STAFF" is set in white italic on the throat.
- Finish: matte or satin black, with a glossier upper hoop (the hoop detail is unconfirmed beyond one review).
- Grommets and bumper: black.
- Handle: black grip.
- Butt cap: red with a white W, in a black surround.
- Head shape: oval, the traditional Pro Staff teardrop. Flat-sided 21.5 mm constant beam.
- Common special edition:
  - Pro Staff 97 v14 Roland-Garros 2026 "Session de Soiree": a navy / midnight frame with a terracotta (clay-orange) grip. It is a limited release, still listed in late 2026.

### Previous: Pro Staff 97 v14 (February 17, 2023)

- Main frame: a colorshift bronze / copper over a near-black base. Approximate mid-tone `#7a4a32`. Highlights on the hoop sides reach copper `#9a6a50`. Wilson described the Pro Staff X v14 sibling as shifting "maroon-to-gold".
- Colorshift: the paint moves between dark espresso / black-brown and copper-bronze. It reads more copper in direct light.
- Top of the head (10 to 2 o'clock): it fades to a dark navy-black (`#22232b`) across the top. This is a soft gradient, not a hard split, and the bumper is also dark.

- Head shape: oval, traditional Pro Staff.

## CLASH 100

### Current: Clash v3 (January 15, 2025)

- Models: 100, 100 Pro, 100L, 108.
- Main frame: Wilson calls it "graphite black". It is a near-black matte, approximate hex `#1c1c1e`.
- Colorshift: none.
- Top of the head: black, the same as the frame (no band).
- Accent color: infrared / red-orange (`#ff4a22`). It sits on:
  - the inner wall of the hoop (the string-bed side, visible from the side and angled views)
  - the underside of the throat bridge
  - small inner grommet tips
  - a clip at about 10 o'clock
- Seen face-on, the frame reads almost all black with a thin orange line inside the hoop.
- Throat: black. Wilson mentions an embossed (tone-on-tone) Clash logo on the throat.
- Wordmark: a large "Wilson" in red-orange (`#ff4a22`) on the outer face of one shaft, with "v3" and "100" marks nearby. This is the most visible color element of the frame.
- Finish: matte.
- Grommets and bumper: black outside. The bumper uses the new "Click & Go" plant-based (Agiplast) system.
- Handle: black grip.
- Butt cap: red with a white W, in a black surround.
- Head shape: oval. The Clash head is a slightly rounder, fuller oval than the Blade, with a rounded-edge beam.
- Common variants:
  - Clash 100 v3 Reverse: a cosmetic variant in bright gloss red-orange (`#ec3a2e`) with a white "Wilson" wordmark and a white grip. Same specs. It is fairly common because it looks like the classic red Clash.
  - US Open 2026 Clash 100 v3: a cream / white frame with a lime-yellow (`#e0ed7f`) upper hoop band, a royal blue wordmark, and a white grip.

## ULTRA 100

## SHIFT 99

- Wilson has released no v2. Wilson's own store lists only "Shift 99 V1" parts, and Tennis Warehouse US had no Shift rackets on its Wilson racquet pages when checked. A 2026 Australian buyer's guide still lists only V1 models. Some eBay listings call a 2025 Roland Garros Shift "V2", but there is no Wilson source for that, so treat it as a seller label.

- Wordmark: a small orange logo block, a dark grey spec label, and "S H I F T" in black spaced capitals down one shaft, with the "I" printed in orange. The shaft "Wilson" is light grey / white and low contrast.
- Finish: gloss pearl.
- Grommets and bumper: white / pearl.
- Handle: light grey grip (`#a0a0a2`).
- Butt cap: red with a black W, in a pearl-grey surround.
- Head shape: oval. The Shift 99 head is a fairly round 99 sq in. 16x20 pattern.
- Special editions: a US Open 2025 Shift 99L exists. It is not common.

## Brand-wide signatures

What makes a racket read as a Wilson at a glance:

1. **The giant shaft wordmark.** A huge lowercase-style "Wilson" script runs along the outer face of one throat shaft, from the bridge almost to the grip. It is the main graphic on every model. In older generations (Blade v9, Clash v2, Ultra v4) it was tone-on-tone and low contrast. In the 2025-26 generation it is a loud contrast color (mint on the Blade, red-orange on the Clash, aqua on the Ultra).
2. **The red butt cap with the W.** The cap is red with a W. In the current generation (Blade v10, Clash v3, Ultra v5, PS Classic) the W is white. The Shift uses a black W on red. In the previous generation (Blade v9, Clash v2, Ultra v4, PS v14) the W was black on red-orange. RF is the only exception, with copper and the RF logo.
3. **The W string stencil.** It is a blocky, flat-topped W centered in the string bed, usually black ink.
4. **One-color frames with a contrasting top band in older generations.** 2022-24 frames had a matte black or navy bumper band from 10 to 2 o'clock (Blade v9, Clash v2, Ultra v4). The 2025-26 frames dropped it and are mostly monochrome heads.
5. **Small "hot spot" accent marks.** A single short contrasting tick on the hoop at about 4 o'clock (infrared on Blade v10, orange on Shift), or a thin stripe (red and yellow on the PS Classic).
6. **Oval heads only.** No Wilson in scope uses a squared isometric head. Use `head: 'oval'` for every line. Blade, Pro Staff, and RF are narrower teardrop ovals. Clash, Ultra, and Shift are slightly fuller and rounder.
7. **Metallic paints.** Most frames use metallic or colorshift paint (Blade, Ultra, Clash v2, PS v14). The flat or matte exceptions are Clash v3, PS Classic, and RF97.

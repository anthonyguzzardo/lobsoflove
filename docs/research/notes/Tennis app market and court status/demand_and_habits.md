# Demand-side evidence: social tennis/pickleball tracking app (US, as of 10 Oct 2026)

Scope note: Figures are dated inline. "2025 data" means calendar-2025 participation published in early 2026 (USTA report 18 Feb 2026; SFIA Topline 12 Mar 2026). App Store review dates shown as "Mon DD" without a year are reviews under 12 months old as of 10 Oct 2026, so the year is 2026 unless noted. Reddit (r/10s, r/Pickleball), X/TikTok and the Strava Community Hub were not fetchable from this environment (Reddit returns a network-security block page; Strava hub returns 403), so forum quotes below come from App Store reviews, Hacker News, Garmin forums, Product Hunt and blogs. That is a real gap, flagged in each section.

## Q1. How many Americans played tennis and pickleball in 2025, how often, how many are 18–34, and is pickleball cannibalizing or feeding tennis?

### Takeaway
Tennis hit a record 27.3M US players in 2025 (+6%, 8.8% of the 6+ population; 14.5M "core" 10+ times/yr; 23 play occasions per player on average) and pickleball hit 24.3M (+22.8%; 7.48M core 8+ times/yr). Roughly 30% of tennis players (~8.2M) are 18–34 and 55% are under 35, but 2025 growth came almost entirely from 35+. Both sports grew together in 2025; the industry's own framing is "broadening, not fragmentation," while court conversions (≥10% of tennis courts) are the real friction point.

### Cited Findings
**Tennis (USTA 2026 report, based on 2025 data; published 18 Feb 2026)**
- 27.3M Americans (6+) played tennis at least once in 2025, +1.6M (+6%) YoY; 8.8% of population; "1 in every 12 Americans"; +54% vs the 2019 baseline of 17.7M — [USTA 2026 U.S. Tennis Participation Report (PDF), p.3–5](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/2026-pdfs/2026-us-tennis-participation-report.pdf); [USTA press release, 18 Feb 2026](https://www.usta.com/en/home/stay-current/national/tennis-participation-continues-to-surge-with-six-consecutive-yea.html)
- Core players (10+ times in past 12 months): 14.5M (+11%), 53% of all players, 93% of all play occasions — [USTA PDF p.4](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/2026-pdfs/2026-us-tennis-participation-report.pdf)
- Play occasions: 616M (+7%), "23 occasions per player, on average" — [USTA PDF p.4](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/2026-pdfs/2026-us-tennis-participation-report.pdf)
- Frequency bands grew across the board: 50+ times/yr segment +~9%; 10–20 and 21–49 times/yr segments +12% and +11% — [USTA PDF p.21](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/2026-pdfs/2026-us-tennis-participation-report.pdf)
- First-time players 4.9M (+9%, record; 18% of all players); retained players 20.7M (+10%; 80% of 2024 players played again — a five-year high); returning lapsed players 1.7M (−26%) — [USTA PDF p.3–4](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/2026-pdfs/2026-us-tennis-participation-report.pdf)
- Age profile of 2025 players (Table 3): 6–12 13%; 13–17 12%; 18–24 12% (index 131 vs population); 25–34 18% (index 123); 35–44 15%; 45–54 12%; 55–64 10%; 65+ 9%. "Players under 35 representing a majority of participants (55%)." 2024 shares for 18–24 and 25–34 were 14% and 19% — [USTA PDF p.12](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/2026-pdfs/2026-us-tennis-participation-report.pdf)
- "Established adults (age 35+) drove nearly 95% of all growth, totaling 1.5 million players"; 35–44 +13% (+488K); 65+ +14% (+299K); female +10% (+1.1M) — [USTA PDF p.3, 5](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/2026-pdfs/2026-us-tennis-participation-report.pdf)
- Income skew: 39% of players have household income $125K+ (index 147); 39% play primarily at a public park (42% in 2024); 13% primarily at school/college (16% in 2024) — [USTA PDF p.12](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/2026-pdfs/2026-us-tennis-participation-report.pdf)
- Male 56% / female 44%; Hispanic/Latino 19% (index 119), Asian/Pacific Islander 11% (index 181), Black 14% — [USTA PDF p.12](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/2026-pdfs/2026-us-tennis-participation-report.pdf)
- Latent demand: 25.4M "very interested" non-players, "approximately one interested non-player for everyone who plays" — [USTA PDF p.22](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/2026-pdfs/2026-us-tennis-participation-report.pdf)
- Southern California section (USTA SoCal): 2.6M players in 2025 (2.5M in 2024; 1.9M in 2020), 6.3% YoY growth; participation rate 11.9% of population in 2025 (11.4% in 2024; 8.5% in 2020) — the highest rate of any USTA section listed — [USTA PDF p.16–17](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/2026-pdfs/2026-us-tennis-participation-report.pdf)
- San Diego ranks #9 nationally for Google search interest in "tennis" in 2025 (was #5 in 2024, #8 in 2020) — [USTA PDF p.10](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/2026-pdfs/2026-us-tennis-participation-report.pdf)
- Other racquet sports in the same dataset: padel 1.1M Americans in 2025 (first year segmented) — [USTA PDF p.11](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/2026-pdfs/2026-us-tennis-participation-report.pdf)
- Methodology: Physical Activity Council (PAC) survey of ~18,000 Americans 6+, supplemented by the USTA Tennis Engagement Study; margin of error 0.2% at 95% CI — [USTA PDF p.2, 23](https://www.usta.com/content/dam/usta/2026-pdfs/2026-us-tennis-participation-report.pdf)
- Skeptic's view: Racket Business (industry newsletter) argues tennis-ball sales imply "actual player count closer to 15–20 million, not 27.3 million" — [Racket Business, 25 Jun 2026](https://racketbusiness.com/p/nobody-chose-this-the-real-story-of-racquet-sports-in-2026) (opinion; unverified)

**Cannibalization vs feeding**

### Inferences
- 18–34 tennis players ≈ 30% × 27.3M ≈ 8.2M (12% + 18%); in 2024 it was ≈ 33% × 25.7M ≈ 8.5M, so the young-adult cohort is roughly flat-to-slightly-down in absolute terms while older adults drove growth. The 18–34 segment is still over-indexed vs population (index 123–131).
- Applying SFIA's 2024 age shares (30% for 18–34) to 24.3M pickleball players implies ~7M 18–34 pickleball players; combined, the 18–34 US racquet-sport pool is on the order of 12–15M (with overlap unknown).
- The core player (10+ times/yr tennis, 8+ pickleball) is the realistic tracking-app user: ~14.5M tennis + ~7.5M pickleball. Core tennis players generate 93% of occasions, so a logging app's engagement would concentrate in roughly half the base.
- Southern California is the single strongest tennis market by penetration (11.9%), and San Diego's heavy public-court and campus supply (free Morley/Balboa pickleball; 13 UCSD tennis + 8 pickleball courts) supports a "who's at the courts" use case.

### Gaps
- No public source breaks pickleball 2025 participation into 18–34 counts; only "13–24 highest participation rate" and 2024 shares.
- No published dual-participation (plays both tennis and pickleball) percentage for 2025; USTA PDF does not report it.
- No San Diego-metro player count for tennis or pickleball (USTA reports at section level only).

## Q2. What do rec players actually use to track matches and hours in 2026?

### Takeaway
There is no survey evidence; the behavioral evidence points to a patchwork: generic wearable workout types (Apple Watch "Tennis", Garmin "Other/Cardio", Strava's tennis/pickleball sport types since Jan 2023), a long tail of tiny iOS score/match loggers (most with <25 ratings), spreadsheets/notes, and group chats for scheduling. Pickleheads (11K App Store ratings, ~1M monthly users) is the only mass-adopted racquet "organizing" tool, and it is pickleball-only and scheduling-centric, not a personal log.

- Strava added Tennis, Pickleball, Racquetball, Squash, Badminton and Table Tennis as sport types in January 2023 (manual/mobile record; no court-specific stats) — [Strava Stories](https://stories.strava.com/articles/more-sport-types-added); [Strava support: supported sport types](https://support.strava.com/hc/en-us/articles/216919407-Supported-Sport-Types-on-Strava)

### Inferences
- For hours/time, the Apple Watch or Garmin workout is the de facto log; for scores/opponents, most rec players keep nothing or a memory/group chat; the only widely adopted app behavior is scheduling (pickleball).
- The App Store long tail (at least a dozen tennis loggers launched 2025–2026, several explicitly "track with friends") shows builders see the gap, but none has crossed 100 ratings, suggesting distribution/network effects, not feature set, are the bottleneck.

### Gaps
- No survey of what share of rec players log matches, or by what method; none found from USTA, TIA, SFIA or Strava.
- Strava has never published tennis/pickleball upload counts; the Year in Sport 2025 report does not break them out.
- Reddit/Talk Tennis "how do you track" threads could not be retrieved.

## Q4. How big is the racket-nerd / stringing hobby, what does gear cost, and are there gear-log apps?

### Takeaway
Racket culture is a large, review-driven niche: Talk Tennis is a multi-million-message forum, Tennisnerd runs a ~58K-subscriber gear channel, Tennis Warehouse publishes playtester reviews and "top reviews of the year."

## Key Question 1: Is there any app or site in 2026 that shows live occupancy of public courts (actual presence, not reservations)? If not, who tried and what happened?

### Takeaway
No global or national "is this court open right now" layer exists in October 2026. There is a crowded field of small, mostly single-developer apps attempting it with four distinct approaches (manual crowd reports, passive geofence check-ins, live cameras, and camera-AI for facilities), none of which shows evidence of scale (App Store listings show 0-2 ratings). The only reliable "availability" data at scale is reservation data from booking platforms (Rec.us, CourtReserve, Playtomic), which measures bookings, not presence.

### Cited Findings

**Manual crowd-report apps (status self-reported by players)**
- CourtCheck: Skip the Wait (iOS, developer "BO TAO", v1.0 released March 3, 2024). Claims "live occupancy updates and access over 70,000 more tennis courts than Google Maps." Status is crowdsourced three ways: user-submitted open/busy/closed updates with optional photos, an "Ask Live Update" request that notifies players near a court, and user-generated content. Free, no in-app purchases listed. App Store shows only 2 ratings (5.0). — [Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/courtcheck-skip-the-wait/id6759684260)
- NearbyCourt (web + iOS + Apple Watch, copyright 2026). Model: check live availability, one-tap check-in/check-out on arrival/departure, plus quick reports with four states ("open, short wait, full, or rained out"); each court page is a "live board" showing which courts are taken, who reported it and how long ago. Claims tennis coverage across 47 metros (NYC, LA, SF, Seattle, etc.), "10,000+" pickleball courts, and hundreds of padel clubs. Free; no user counts or founding team disclosed. — [NearbyCourt](https://nearbycourt.com/)
- LiveCourt: in waitlist/early-access mode only ("Reserve your spot on the list and be first when we launch"); promises "every public court in your area, one map" with community-updated live status. No launch, pricing, or founder info. — [LiveCourt](https://livecourt.app/)
- CourtSeeker: surfaced in search as "real-time, crowdsourced occupancy for tennis and pickleball courts," but its site returned HTTP 404 on 2026-10-10 and a follow-up search found no listing, suggesting it is defunct or never launched. — [search result snippet](https://www.courtseeker.com/) (404)

**Passive geofence check-in apps (presence inferred from phone location of app users)**
- PickledIn (developer Altum, LLC; latest version 2.1.0 on August 7, 2025). "See who's playing pickleball right now - before you head to the court." Uses geofencing to auto check users in/out on arrival/departure and shows live player counts per court. Free. App Store: "not enough ratings or reviews to display." — [Apple App Store (MX listing)](https://apps.apple.com/mx/app/pickledin/id1664217147)
- Paddle Stack App (developer Capital Q Design LLC). "Live pickleball court tracker" using automatic background geofencing, "zero check-ins"; dashboard shows "Active Players," "Open Courts," and "Paddles Stacked" (players waiting). Claims location data is never stored. Free. App Store: 1 rating, 1.0 stars. — [Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/paddle-stack-app/id6760633451)
- PaddleTaps (iOS): search snippet describes real-time court availability with a waitlist that notifies when a court frees up; not independently verified. — [search result](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6633439727)

**Live camera apps (presence visible on video)**
- ParkViewLive (developer ParkView Cam Inc.; v1.0 released June 13, 2025; v1.5.4 on October 3, 2025). "Real-time cameras for pickleball, tennis, and volleyball courts, beaches, and more, so you can see availability, crowds, and conditions before leaving home." Free with ads; not enough ratings to display. — [Apple App Store (IN listing)](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/parkviewlive/id6744260415)
- ParkViewLive site (copyright 2026) lists "123 Featured locations" and a "Host a Camera" program, implying privately hosted cameras rather than city partnerships; no business model or city list is disclosed. — [ParkViewLive](https://parkviewlive.com/)

**Camera + AI occupancy for facilities (B2B)**
- VisioCourt (copyright 2026, "Presented at MIT"): uses existing IP cameras or turnkey hardware with a "purpose-built human-detection model" handling occlusion, glare, and shadows; "privacy first, no facial recognition." Features: real-time per-court occupancy, estimated wait times, utilization analytics, ML forecasting, a facility dashboard, and a player app with check-ins. Covers tennis, pickleball, padel, badminton, basketball, volleyball. Only named deployment: Oakwell Courts, Cary NC (32 courts). Sold as a 10-day risk-free pilot; pricing undisclosed. — [VisioCourt](https://visiocourt.com/)

**Performance-camera vendors (PlaySight, Wingfield, SwingVision, PB Vision) are not availability products**
- PlaySight: multi-camera smart-court video, live streaming and analytics for teams/clubs; in 2025-26 it integrated "PlaySight SmartCourt AI Video & Insights" into CourtReserve so players can capture/replay video within bookings. Nothing in its materials describes occupancy detection or public availability feeds. — [CourtReserve announcement](https://courtreserve.com/?p=17726); [ITF PAT approval report](https://m.itftennis.com/media/6220/pat-13-003-pat-approval-report-playsight-rev-0.pdf)
- Wingfield: ITF-approved tracking system with two cameras above the net post and one at the baseline connected to a "BOX" processing unit on the net post; purpose is shot/player tracking, not occupancy. — [ITF PAT report for Wingfield](https://m.itftennis.com/media/6223/pat-19-019-pat-approval-report-wingfield-final-rev-0.pdf)
- SwingVision and PB Vision analyze recorded or single-camera match video for stats/line calls; neither offers live court-availability viewing. — [SwingVision App Store](https://apps.apple.com/US/app/id989461317); [PB Vision listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/pb-vision/6467020610)

**Reservation-based "availability" at scale (bookings, not presence)**
- CourtReserve measures utilization purely from bookings: "Percentage of bookable court hours with a booking during the selected period"; multi-court events count as fully booked "even if the event ran underfilled"; no check-in or physical-presence data is used. — [CourtReserve Pulse Utilization tab](https://intercom.help/courtreserve/en/articles/15279799-courtreserve-pulse-utilization-tab)
- CourtReserve (founded 2016, St. Augustine FL) reports 2,300+ facilities and 6M+ players, offers a "secure web API (HTTPS/JSON)" for memberships/reservations/events, has "Public booking (no account required)," and a "CourtReserve Network" cross-club discovery product in Florida beta; raised $54M from Mainsail Partners on October 21, 2025. Page last updated September 2026. — [CourtReserve AI info page](https://courtreserve.com/ai-info/)
- SF Rec & Park's Rec.us exposes a public, unauthenticated REST API (`api.rec.us/v1/*`) returning per-court schedules with slot status RESERVABLE / RESERVATION / OPEN, pricing, and booking policies; no bot protection observed. Booking itself requires a Firebase token, contract acceptance, and a Stripe card. — [browse.sh skill notes on rec.us](https://www.browse.sh/skills/rec.us/sf-court-booking-c8l5z9)
- Spotery (SF's prior system, 2019-2024) failed in part because it "was unable to provide a real-time check-in system that would have helped identify users who routinely failed to show up," and users created multiple email accounts to hoard bookings; SF switched to Rec.us in April 2024, which ties accounts to a phone number. — [Tennis Coalition SF search summary](https://www.tenniscoalitionsf.org/?p=5804)
- NYC: a third-party site, nyctenniscourts.com, exists for NYC public court info but returned HTTP 503 during research; contents unverified. — [nyctenniscourts.com](https://nyctenniscourts.com/)

**Crowdsourced court directories (static data, not live status)**
- Pickleheads is USA Pickleball's official court and game finder; it lists 16,000+ court locations in the US and Canada, grew "almost entirely from crowdsourced edits from players — over 60,000 to date," receives 250+ edits per week, covers 6,600 cities, and reports 700k+ unique visitors per month; USA Pickleball's 2,200 ambassadors maintain court/schedule info there. It does not show live occupancy. — [Pickleheads blog](https://www.pickleheads.com/blog/usa-pickleball); [Pickleball Union](https://pickleballunion.com/pickleheads-is-usa-pickleballs-new-official-court-and-game-finder/)
- Bounce (bounce.game) positions itself as a marketplace/"demand generation" layer (free listing, paid Growth/Scale plans, no booking commission) that complements CourtReserve, PlayByPoint, ClubCloud, AllBooked and PodPlay; it lists courts and open play but not live presence. — [Bounce blog, July 28, 2026](https://www.bounce.game/blog/padel-booking-software)

### Inferences
- Four technical approaches have been tried and none has achieved critical mass: (1) manual reports suffer from cold-start (nobody reports until there are users); (2) geofence auto check-in only counts app users, so counts are wrong until penetration is high at a given court; (3) cameras solve accuracy but require per-court hardware and host permission; (4) camera-AI (VisioCourt) is being sold B2B to facility managers, not to the public-court problem.
- The near-zero rating counts across CourtCheck, Paddle Stack, PickledIn and ParkViewLive (all launched 2024-2025) indicate the category is unproven, not saturated. There is no evidence any of these has a San Diego presence.
- The most defensible "availability" layer today is reservation data, and it is already scrapable in a few cities (SF via Rec.us public API; CivicRec and rec.us via unofficial APIs). But reservation data cannot reveal walk-on crowding or no-shows, which is the actual pain for public-court players.

### Gaps
- No usage or retention numbers for any crowdsourced status app could be found; founders and funding for NearbyCourt, LiveCourt, VisioCourt are undisclosed.
- Could not confirm whether any city parks department has ever purchased occupancy sensors or cameras for courts; no such procurement surfaced.
- CourtSeeker's history (who built it, when it died) could not be determined (site 404).

## Key Question 2: How do the top-20 US cities handle public tennis/pickleball court reservations and walk-on rules in 2026? Which systems have public APIs or scrapable schedules?

### Takeaway
There is no standard: NYC uses seasonal permits plus first-come sign-up with online reservations at a few courts; SF uses Rec.us (public API) for a minority of courts with the rest walk-up; LA uses paid online reservations (Vermont Systems RecTrac) on reservable courts with free first-come "open play" courts; Denver, Dallas and DC keep pickleball drop-in only; Seattle books via ActiveNet with drop-ins yielding to reservation holders. Only Rec.us (SF) was confirmed to expose a public, unauthenticated schedule API; CivicRec has an unofficial API; most others are web portals that would require scraping or per-city integration.

### Cited Findings
- **New York City (NYC Parks):** 2026 season permits went on sale February 2026; permit required from the first Saturday in April through the Sunday before Thanksgiving; full-season adult (18-61) $100, seniors (62+) $20, under-18 $10, single-play $15, 10% IDNYC discount. "At a few popular courts, online reservations are offered at $15 per court, per hour," bookable up to seven days ahead until midnight the day before; "For all other courts, please visit the tennis court facility in-person with your permit to reserve a court for use on the same day." — [NYC Parks tennis permits (via search summary; direct fetch returned 403)](https://www.nycgovparks.org/permits/tennis-permits)
- Prospect Park Tennis Center (Brooklyn): permit required for daytime outdoor play 6am-7pm from May 17 through October 4, 2026; reservations through an Aptus Soft portal (prospectpark.aptussoft.com/member); evening 7-11pm courts need no permit and are bookable a week ahead. — [Prospect Park Alliance](https://www.prospectpark.org/visit-the-park/places-to-go/tennis-center/tennis-permits)
- A March 30, 2025 player essay describes NYC as "fragmented": ~500 public courts for 8 million residents, single-play passes that "have to be physically mailed to you in advance," paper sign-up sheets at many courts, court hoarding past the one-hour courtesy limit, and the subtitle "To play tennis in New York City, you have to be in the know." — [Jack Riewe, Substack](https://jackriewe.substack.com/p/the-art-of-the-tennis-court-reservation)
- **San Francisco (SF Rec & Park):** Online reservations began as a Spotery pilot on April 12, 2019 (21 tennis + 6 pickleball courts at 11 parks; free; 1.5-hour slots; max 1/day and 3/week; up to one week ahead). — [Tennis Coalition SF, 2019](https://www.tenniscoalitionsf.org/?p=4420). Spotery grew to 67 courts, then was replaced by Rec.us in April 2024 because it could not do real-time check-in and allowed multi-account booking abuse. — [Tennis Coalition SF](https://www.tenniscoalitionsf.org/?p=5804). On May 16, 2024 the Rec & Park Commission voted unanimously for a $5/hour online reservation fee at 28 reservable courts, leaving 38 of 66 locations walk-up first-come; the fee was "part of a plan to prevent frequent overbooking of the courts—which often results in conflicts." — [KTVU, May 16, 2024](https://ktvu.com/news/pickleball-could-cost-you-5-at-these-san-francisco-courts). SF's current terms: bring the confirmation, name must match ID, no more than 1 court/day or 3/week, no lessons/clinics. — [SF Rec & Park terms](https://sfrecpark.org/1853/Tennis-and-Pickleball-Terms-and-Conditio)
- Rec.us public API (SF): `/v1/locations/availability?publishedSites=true&organizationSlug=san-francisco-rec-park`, `/v1/locations/{id}/schedule?startDate=...`, `/v1/price?...`; slot statuses RESERVABLE/RESERVATION/OPEN; reservation windows vary per court (typically 7 days; e.g., Alice Marble Court 4 only 2 days); slots release at 08:00 local by default. — [browse.sh](https://www.browse.sh/skills/rec.us/sf-court-booking-c8l5z9). A Browserbase/Stagehand template exists to automate SF tennis/pickleball bookings via the web UI. — [Browserbase template](https://www.browserbase.com/templates/pickleball)
- **Los Angeles (LA Rec & Parks):** Online reservations 24 hours a day, no more than one week ahead and up to one hour before start; weekday 7am-5pm $8/hour, weekday after 5pm and weekends/holidays $12/hour; paddle tennis $12/hour; permit fee $15/court/hour. "Open play courts are free and are first come, first served." — [LA Parks tennis reservation page (search summary; page 403 on fetch)](https://recreation.parks.lacity.gov/node/996). LA's recreation software vendor is RecTrac LLC d/b/a Vermont Systems. — [search summary citing LA Parks board report](https://dev.pastilla.recreation.parks.lacity.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/commissioner/2022/feb17/22-030.pdf). LA Parks' pickleball page (fetch 403) was summarized as four dedicated lighted pickleball courts and 34 shared-use courts, with eight more dedicated courts under development (date unknown). — [laparks.org/pickleball (search summary)](https://www.laparks.org/pickleball)
- **Chicago (Park District):** "Pickleball Mania" targeted 200 courts by end of 2025; courts in 27 parks with 36 more underway (date of statement not shown); some facilities (e.g., Fosco Park) allow reservations. No 2026 reservation rules found. — [Pickleball Union](https://pickleballunion.com/chicagos-pickleball-mania-project-sets-amazing-200-court-target-over-the-next-two-years/); [Pickleheads Fosco Park](https://www.pickleheads.com/courts/us/illinois/chicago/fosco-park)
- **Seattle (Seattle Parks & Recreation):** Reservations via ActiveNet (activecommunities.com) must be made at least 24 hours and up to two weeks ahead, no same-day; unbooked courts are drop-in first-come for 90 minutes max, and "if someone shows up with proof of reservation, drop-ins must yield the court." — [search summary of seattle.gov/parks/sports/pickleball](https://www.seattle.gov/parks/sports/pickleball). May 30, 2024: SPR designated Green Lake East Courts as the dedicated pickleball hub and Lower Woodland as the tennis hub, cancelled Lincoln Park pickleball, added noise fencing at Miller and Laurelhurst, piloted "Quiet-Paddles" (50% or less of standard acoustic footprint) at Belltown, and said it was "re-vamping the online reservation system." — [Seattle Parkways, May 30, 2024](https://parkways.seattle.gov/2024/05/30/implementation-of-the-2022-outdoor-pickleball-study-and-the-2024-seattle-parks-recreation-pickleball-and-tennis-vision/). In April 2026 SPR circulated a "draft racquet sports strategy" for feedback; the community survey found 60% of tennis players said pickleball growth made it harder to find tennis courts. — [West Seattle Blog, April 2026 (search summary; fetch 403)](https://westseattleblog.com/2026/04/where-to-play-tennis-where-to-play-pickleball-seattle-parks-asks-for-feedback-on-draft-racquet-sports-strategy/)
- **Denver:** Tennis courts reserved via Active Communities with seasonal permits (winter Oct 24-Mar 14 is drop-in only); USTA-sanctioned groups get first priority; "One drop-in court will remain open for drop-in users at all times"; "all pickleball and futsal courts are available for drop-in use only. Pickleball play is strictly prohibited on Tennis Courts." — [Denver Parks & Recreation](https://www.denvergov.org/Government/Agencies-Departments-Offices/Agencies-Departments-Offices-Directory/Parks-Recreation/Park-Permits/Athletic-Fields-Courts)
- **Dallas:** 40+ venues; 8 parks with 28 dedicated pickleball courts; 24+ parks with lined tennis courts; "Outdoor courts are free to use and available on a first-come, first-served basis"; rotate off after one game if others wait; three contractor-run tennis centers (Fretz 4, Samuell Grand 12, LB Houston 12) require paid reservations through a Vermont Systems "myvscloud" portal. — [Dallas Parks](https://dallasparks.org/578/Pickleball)
- **Washington, DC:** DPR hosts pickleball at 19 facilities (1,500+ participants FY23) and lines some tennis courts for first-come pickleball. — [search summary](https://www.popville.com/2026/05/pickleball-part-two-washington-dc-problems/). A May 15, 2026 PoPville post documents players installing nets on unlined courts against DPR rules; commenters: "DPR will not allow pickleball to be played in any spaces that do not have lines drawn for this sport" and "the pickleball players are continuously breaking the rules leading to frustration from tennis players." — [PoPville, May 15, 2026](https://www.popville.com/2026/05/pickleball-part-two-washington-dc-problems/)
- **Boston:** search summary describes a "Play Local" app at four tennis sites (Ringer Playground, Moakley Park, Cassidy Playground, Boston Common) with free sign-up; the city page returned 404, so currency is unverified. — [content.boston.gov (404)](https://content.boston.gov/node/19111)
- **Austin:** nine free parks pickleball locations, "some locations are first-come, first serve, but others require a reservation"; Austin Tennis Center charges $6 singles / $12 doubles. — [6AM City Austin guide](https://atxtoday.6amcity.com/city-guide/play/where-to-play-pickleball-austin-tx)
- **Vendor APIs:** CivicRec (CivicPlus Recreation Management, 1,200+ installations) has an unofficial API (Supergood) that pulls facility bookings and pushes reservations using staff login/session management. — [Supergood CivicRec API docs](https://supergood.ai/docs/civicrec-api). ActiveNet: Santa Monica case study exists but no public availability API surfaced. — [ActiveNet Santa Monica case study, 2018](https://info.activenetwork.com/Assets/Active+Network/ACTIVE+Net/Customer+Stories/Santa+Monica+Case+study-06-08-2018-Active-net-.pdf)

### Inferences
- Walk-on remains the dominant access mode for public courts even in cities with reservation systems (SF: 38 of 66 locations walk-up; LA: open-play courts; Denver/Dallas/DC: pickleball drop-in only). That means a reservation-feed product covers at best a minority of courts and the largest pain (walk-on uncertainty) is unaddressed everywhere.
- SF's Rec.us is the cleanest data source found and would be a natural first integration; San Diego has no equivalent public-court reservation feed (see KQ3/San Diego section).
- The pattern of cities tightening rules (fees to curb overbooking, phone-number-bound accounts, drop-ins yielding to reservations) shows no-shows and hoarding are system-level problems that a presence signal could help with.

### Gaps
- Did not verify 2026 rules for Houston, Phoenix, Philadelphia, San Antonio, San Jose, Jacksonville, Columbus, Charlotte, Indianapolis, Fort Worth, or Boston (page 404); a city-by-city audit remains to be done.
- No public API documentation was found for ActiveNet, RecDesk, or Vermont Systems; scraping feasibility for those portals is untested.
- NYC's online-reservation vendor for the "few popular courts" was not identified (the nycgovparks pages returned 403/401).

## Key Question 3: What does UCSD Recreation require to use its tennis and pickleball courts, and is there a public schedule? Plus San Diego public/club specifics.

### Takeaway
UCSD courts are members-only: a Student Membership (auto-imported), Full Access Membership, or a 1-Day/30-Day pass is required; bookings open three days ahead at 12:00 AM, are one hour, one per member per day, with one free guest; the booking portal (rec.ucsd.edu/booking) requires sign-in so there is no public schedule. City of San Diego public tennis is mostly run by nonprofit clubs under Special Use Permits (Balboa, La Jolla, Peninsula, Barnes, Pacific Beach) with their own day passes and booking windows, while city pickleball is largely free, first-come, and spread across indoor gym "open play" hours and lined outdoor courts; no city-wide online court reservation feed exists.

### Cited Findings

**UCSD Recreation**
- Inventory: Muir 4 tennis + 4 pickleball; Warren 1 tennis + 4 pickleball; Coast 1 tennis (unlighted); North Campus 7 tennis with bleacher seating for 210. "All of our tennis courts except Coast are lighted." Booking link goes to rec.ucsd.edu/booking. — [UCSD Recreation outdoor courts page](https://recreation.ucsd.edu/facilities/outdoor-courts-and-sporting-sites/)
- Eligibility: "You may book a court with an active Full Access Membership, 1-Day Day Pass, 30-Day Day Pass, or Student Membership (automatic campus import). Memberships and Day Passes must already be active on the day you access the booking portal." — [UCSD Recreation policies](https://recreation.ucsd.edu/policies/)
- Window and limits: "Court bookings open three (3) days in advance at 12:00 AM." Outdoor tennis and pickleball: one-hour slots, max one booking per member per day (racquetball/squash: two per day). Each tennis/pickleball booking includes one complimentary guest who needs no separate check-in. Cancelled times "immediately become available for other eligible members." Enforcement for "repeated no-shows, misuse of bookings" can include suspension. No walk-on/drop-in rule for unbooked courts is stated. — [UCSD Recreation policies](https://recreation.ucsd.edu/policies/)
- Day pass prices: 1 Day $20 (Staff & Faculty) / $25 (Community & Triton Affiliates); 30 Days $44 / $55. Membership benefits list "Tennis courts (reservations required)." — [UCSD Recreation memberships](https://recreation.ucsd.edu/memberships/)
- Booking portal lists Tennis, Pickleball, Squash, Racquetball; requires "Sign In"; at fetch time it displayed "Bookings Suspended." No schedule is visible without authentication. — [rec.ucsd.edu/booking](https://rec.ucsd.edu/booking)
- Conflicting third-party claims: aggregator sites say reservations are "up to 48 hours in advance" by phone (858) 534-3557; the official policy page says three days. Prefer the official page. — [third-party page](https://fis-snd-nav-1.ucsc.edu/when-can-i-reserve-ucsd-courts-get-your-spot-now) contradicted by [UCSD policies](https://recreation.ucsd.edu/policies/)

## Key Question 6: Precedents for crowdsourced "status" layers (Waze, Surfline cams, Google Popular Times, Waitz) and what made them sustainable

### Takeaway
Every durable live-status product combined a passive signal (GPS traces, Location History, Bluetooth/Wi-Fi sniffing, fixed cameras) with a light active layer, and each was funded by something other than the status data itself (ads for Waze/Google, subscriptions for Surfline, institutional B2B contracts for Waitz/Occuspace). Purely manual crowd reporting has no large-scale precedent.

### Cited Findings
- Waze: founded 2006 (FreeMap Israel) / 2008; collects "anonymous information, including users' speed and location" passively while users actively report accidents, jams and police and earn profile points; ~50M users at Google's June 2013 acquisition for $1.3B, ~130M by 2019; model worked because passive data improved routing for everyone, the app was free, revenue came from hyperlocal ads, and gamification sustained reporting. — [Wikipedia: Waze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waze)
- Google Popular Times / Live busyness (Oct 15, 2020): computed from "aggregated and anonymized Location History data from people who have opted" in; live mode shows when current data deviates from the historical pattern; Google withholds data "if our systems don't have enough information to provide an accurate, anonymous busyness recommendation"; differential privacy applied; users also answer quick post-visit surveys. — [Google Maps 101](https://blog.google/products/maps/maps101-popular-times-and-live-busyness-information/); [Google support](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/11323117?hl=en-IN)
- Surfline: began 1985 as a pay-per-call surf report; its network is "more than 500 cameras"; Premium rose from $99/yr to $119.99 in April 2025 and a $69.99 "Premium with Ads" tier was added in late 2025; estimated ~$550K/month app revenue; reviewers describe it as "a camera subscription with a good forecast attached." — [Breakfinder review](https://breakfinder.surf/en/blog/is-surfline-worth-it); [screensdesign estimate](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/surfline-wave-surf-reports); [SurferToday history](https://www.surfertoday.com/surfing/the-story-of-surfline)
- Waitz / Occuspace: plug-in sensors passively read ambient Bluetooth LE and Wi-Fi signals and run ML to estimate headcount (no lens, no personal data), 80-95% accuracy; one sensor covers ~2,500 sq ft; roughly $600/year for <5,000 sq ft and $8,000-$25,000/year for a university library; founded by four college students; deployed at UCLA, Purdue, Columbia, McGill, Vanderbilt and others; "hundreds of thousands of college students a day." — [McGill Reporter](https://reporter.mcgill.ca/know-before-you-go-with-waitz/); [Campus Technology, Nov 17, 2022](https://campustechnology.com/articles/2022/11/17/occuspace-expands-occupancy-monitoring-to-more-colleges-and-universities.aspx); [Occuspace pricing search summary](https://yespress.io/occuspace)
- Pickleheads (static directory) is the closest racquet-sport precedent: 60,000+ crowdsourced edits, 250+/week, sustained by becoming USA Pickleball's official finder and by its ambassador network. — [Pickleheads](https://www.pickleheads.com/blog/usa-pickleball)

### Inferences
- For courts, the Occuspace model (Bluetooth/Wi-Fi density sensor, ~$600/yr per site) is the cheapest passive signal that does not need video or city permission beyond mounting; a solo founder could pilot one sensor at a high-traffic San Diego site (Pacific Beach Rec, Robb Field) to validate "open vs full" inference before any app growth.
- Surfline's economics suggest a cam-per-court subscription is viable only where demand is intense and the site is iconic; a handful of San Diego hubs (Barnes, Robb Field, Lake Murray, PB Rec, Morley Field) fit that profile better than long-tail neighborhood courts.
- Google withholds live busyness below a privacy threshold; small courts will rarely cross it, which is why Popular Times is unreliable for courts and why an independent signal is still needed.

### Gaps
- Surfline's actual subscriber count and whether Google's Popular Times covers specific tennis facilities were not verified.
- No precedent was found for a crowdsourced status layer in sports facilities that succeeded without either hardware or a platform owner (e.g., a parks department) as anchor.

## Key Question 7: What could a solo founder (Expo/React Native + AI, San Diego, able to visit courts) build?

### Takeaway
The gap is a San Diego-first "open right now" layer that fuses (a) manual reports and passive geofence check-ins, (b) scraped or partnered reservation feeds where they exist (Rec.us-style public APIs, CourtReserve public booking pages, UCSD only via partnership), and (c) a small number of founder-seeded cameras or Bluetooth density sensors at the 5-10 busiest hubs; the evidence shows nobody has reached scale, that San Diego has no reservation feed to piggyback on, and that both tennis and pickleball constituencies (and the city, after the drone/court-log fight) want impartial occupancy data.

### Cited Findings
- All current crowdsourced apps show App Store ratings of 0-2 as of their listings (CourtCheck 2, Paddle Stack 1, PickledIn and ParkViewLive "not enough ratings"). — [CourtCheck](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/courtcheck-skip-the-wait/id6759684260); [Paddle Stack](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/paddle-stack-app/id6760633451); [PickledIn](https://apps.apple.com/mx/app/pickledin/id1664217147); [ParkViewLive](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/parkviewlive/id6744260415)

- Rec.us (SF) proves a parks reservation system can expose per-court slot status publicly without auth. — [browse.sh](https://www.browse.sh/skills/rec.us/sf-court-booking-c8l5z9)
- CourtReserve offers public booking pages and a JSON API, and in 2026 is building a cross-club "Network" discovery layer (Florida beta), signaling that the incumbent sees discovery as the next frontier. — [CourtReserve](https://courtreserve.com/ai-info/)
- Occuspace sensors cost roughly $600/year for small areas and need only a wall outlet. — [search summary](https://yespress.io/occuspace)
- San Diego hubs with documented heavy demand and no reservation feed: Pacific Beach Rec (8 courts, first-come, "arrive early"), Robb Field ("one of the busiest community pickleball rotations"), Waterfront Park (2 free courts downtown with "limited existing courts ... in high demand"). — [Club Recess 2026](https://clubrecess.com/blogs/pickleball/where-to-play-pickleball-in-san-diego-the-2026-edit); [City Robb Field page (search summary)](https://www.sandiego.gov/park-and-recreation/centers/recctr/robb); [San Diego Sun, Aug 13, 2026](https://thesandiegosun.com/the-plan-to-put-pickleball-courts-on-the-sd-convention-center-roof/)
- UCSD courts are bookable only by members through a sign-in portal, with one-hour slots released 3 days ahead at midnight. — [UCSD policies](https://recreation.ucsd.edu/policies/)

### Inferences
- A founder in San Diego can seed ground truth by physically logging occupancy at ~10 hubs over a few weeks, then use that to train a per-court hourly prior (a "Popular Times" for courts) that is useful even before live reports exist; live reports and geofence check-ins then adjust the prior. This directly addresses the cold-start problem that sank earlier apps.
- The UCSD campus is a natural closed beta: a known population (students with automatic memberships), 23 courts across four sites, a booking rule set that creates predictable release times (midnight, 3 days out), and no public schedule. A "Waitz for courts" on campus mirrors a product students already use for libraries.
- Policy value is a second wedge: the city's 2022 report and the drone episode show that utilization data decides court allocation; an impartial occupancy dataset could be offered to the Parks department and community recreation groups (e.g., Rancho Peñasquitos CRG debated time-of-day signage for Hilltop courts in July 2026). — [City notice, July 23, 2026](https://www.sandiego.gov/sites/default/files/2026-07/ranchopenasquitoscrga20260723.pdf)

### Gaps
- No pricing or willingness-to-pay data for a court-status product exists; the only monetization precedents are adjacent (Surfline subscriptions, Occuspace B2B).
- Legal/permission questions for mounting cameras or sensors on city park property were not researched.
- Whether UCSD Recreation would partner on a booking-data feed or permit a campus pilot is unknown.

# Golf apps and other social amateur-sports apps as analogs for a social tennis app (as of October 10, 2026)

Scope: consumer golf apps (18Birdies, TheGrint, Golfshot, Golf GameBook, Hole19, Arccos, Golf Pad, GHIN, SwingU, Lightspeed/Chronogolf, Golf Genius) plus Strava, Zwift, Topgolf/Toptracer, bowling/darts/pool league tools, pickup-basketball finders, Playtomic, and Sleeper. Every figure is dated. Third-party revenue estimates (Owler, ZoomInfo, Sensor Tower, Growjo) are flagged as estimates; none of the golf-app companies disclose audited revenue.

## Key question 1: Which golf-app features correlate with paid conversion and retention, and what numbers are disclosed?

### Takeaway
No golf app discloses conversion or retention rates. What they *do* reveal through pricing and feature gating is consistent: free GPS and scorecard are the acquisition hook; what sits behind the paywall is personal performance analytics (strokes gained, auto shot-tracking, AI caddie/coach, handicap benchmarking, green maps, smartwatch). Social/competition features (live leaderboards, friend games, challenges, king-of-the-course) are almost always free because they are the viral/retention layer, not the payment layer.

### Cited Findings
- 18Birdies Premium is $99.99/year, $19.99/month, or $7.99/week (knowledge base, current as of 2026) — [18Birdies Help](https://help.18birdies.com/article/96-where-can-i-download-the-app-and-how-much-does-it-cost)
- Golf Pad's July 23, 2026 head-to-head: Golf Pad Premium $29.99–$49.99/yr vs 18Birdies Premium $99.99/yr. GPS distances and live leaderboards are free on both; shot tracking is Premium-only on 18Birdies; green contour maps and smartwatch are Premium on both; 18Birdies bundles an AI caddie and side games; Golf Pad has USGA handicap integration (Premium) and 18Birdies does not — [Golf Pad vs 18Birdies, Jul 23 2026](https://golfpadgps.com/news/golf-pad-vs-18birdies)
- 18Birdies' free 2024 year-in-review ("End-of-Year Summary") surfaces each golfer's top 3 courses played, total rounds, highest/lowest handicap, best/average score; a "Scoring Goals for 2025" feature sets custom targets and sends celebration notifications; both are free to all users (Jan 23, 2025) — [18Birdies Clubhouse](https://18birdies.com/clubhouse/community/teeing-up-for-2025-reflect-celebrate-and-plan-with-18-birdies)
- Golfshot Pro is $79.99/yr or $17.99/mo, with a $99.99/yr "Champions" tier; Golfshot claims GPS on 47,000+ courses, auto shot tracking, handicap posting and strokes-gained analytics — [Shotzoom help](https://shotzoom.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000956554-What-is-Golfshot-Pro-and-what-does-it-cost); [Golfshot App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/golfshot-golf-gps-range-finder/id622851626)
- Hole19 (Lisbon, founded 2011) claims 4.8M+ golfers and 42,000+ courses; its "Intelligence" plan is €99.99/yr; free tier includes GPS yardages, basic shot tracking, game modes and course discovery; paid tier includes auto shot detection, strokes gained, "Otto" AI round reports and handicap benchmark comparison; "LivePlay leaderboards" are the social feature — [Hole19 product page](https://www.hole19golf.com/hole19)
- Golf GameBook (Espoo, Finland) claims "over 1.5 million golfers with 50 million rounds"; users "create their own golf challenges and compete with friends on categories like most birdies, best scores, and most rounds" — [Golf GameBook App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/golf-gamebook-scorecard-gps/id409307935). Its model is freemium with advertising plus a paid "gold membership" that removes ads (Sept 25, 2024) — [Golf Business Monitor](https://golfbusinessmonitor.com/golf-equipment/2024/09/golf-gamebook-received-e2-5-million-investment.html)
- Arccos operates hardware + subscription: ~$99/yr after year one for sensor users; the sensor-less "Arccos Air" launched at $199.99/yr; Arccos says it has collected data from 1.5B+ shots and has "hundreds of thousands" of users — [Arccos Air](https://www.arccosgolf.com/products/air); [Golf Hub pricing](https://golfhubz.com/arcocccos-subscription-costs-and-pricing/); [Hartford Business](https://hartfordbusiness.com/article/backed-by-pga-tour-stamford-golf-tech-firm-arccos-uses-data-to-reshape-performance-tracking/)
- TheGrint's 2025 "State of the Union" (CEO Jose Torbay) lists the community features it invested in: a rebuilt GPS with customizable widgets, a Season Replay redesign, "King of the Course" (a weekly per-course leaderboard), "Scorephotos" (shareable moment content), an upcoming "Trophy Room," a public "TheGrint Connect" API (first partner RapidTee tee times), and expansion of "TheGrint Tour" (amateur competition series, new cities incl. Nashville). It cites a "crazy number of downloads" and "millions of rounds" but no exact figures — [TheGrint 2025 State of the Union](https://www.thegrint.com/range/post/2025-state-of-the-union)
- Golf Genius, which now owns Golfshot and SwingU, describes the combination as "the largest customer base of paid subscribers among all suppliers of golf GPS apps" (June 6, 2024) — [Golf Retailing](https://www.golfretailing.com/news/golf-genius-acquires-swing-by-swing-golf/)
- USGA's upgraded GHIN app (Oct 5, 2026) adds "GHIN Challenges": net-score leaderboards created by Allied Golf Associations for daily-fee and municipal courses; 36 AGAs participate, 750+ Challenges created, 30,000+ golfers involved. Non-members get a 30-day free trial — [First Call Golf, Oct 5 2026](https://www.firstcallgolf.com/industry-news/release/2026-10-05/usga-unveils-upgraded-ghin-mobile-app-offers-easier-to-use-interface-and-innovative-features)
- 3.68M US golfers with a Handicap Index posted a record 82M scores in 2025 (USGA, Jan 2026) — [USGA](https://www.usga.org/content/usga/home-page/articles/2026/01/golf-boomed-2025-more-than-82-million-rounds-posted.html)
- Third-party estimates for 18Birdies: Sensor Tower estimated ~60k downloads and ~$1.0M revenue in the prior month (US iOS, 2026) and Owler/ZoomInfo-type profiles put 2025 revenue at ~$15.9M. These are modeled estimates, not disclosures — [Sensor Tower](https://app.sensortower.com/overview/892700751?country=US); [Owler](https://www.owler.com/company/18birdies)

### Inferences
- Across six apps the paywall line is drawn in the same place: "things about *my* game" (shot-by-shot analytics, AI coaching, handicap vs. benchmark, maps) are paid; "things about *us*" (live leaderboards, friend challenges, course leaderboards, year-in-review) are free. The implication is that social features drive engagement/retention and distribution, and personal-improvement analytics drive payment.
- Price anchoring in golf has converged on ~$80–100/yr for a full-featured app and $30–50/yr for a value tier; Arccos shows golfers will pay ~$200/yr when hardware or automatic capture removes the logging burden.
- The USGA entering course-level net leaderboards (GHIN Challenges) in Oct 2026 validates "venue as a leaderboard" but also means golf's governing body now competes with the startups on that feature.

### Gaps
- No golf app publishes free-to-paid conversion, D30 retention, or churn. Industry profiles (Owler, Growjo, Sensor Tower) give modeled estimates only.
- I could not find any A/B evidence that leaderboards or badges specifically lifted retention; the evidence is structural (what is kept free) rather than measured.

## Key question 2: How do golf apps do side games / play-money and small-money bets, and how do they stay legal in the US?

### Takeaway
The dominant pattern is "scorekeeper, not bookmaker": the app calculates Skins/Nassau/Wolf outcomes and hands settlement to Venmo/PayPal/Zelle/Cash App, so the app never touches money. Two real-money attempts exist (LoopGolf; Calcutta via TheGrint's API, using USDC and smart contracts), but Venmo's own terms prohibit gambling payouts, so settlement-by-P2P-app sits in a gray zone. No app I found has built a distinctive *play-money* (points-only) side-bet product; the market solved for real money among friends instead.

### Cited Findings
- The Skins App (free) supports 13 games (Stroke Play, Match Play, Nassau, Nine Point, Banker, Wolf, Hammer, Skins...), auto-allocates handicap strokes, computes payouts at round end, and lets users settle via "Venmo, CashApp, PayPal or Zelle"; the app does not handle money (Golf.com, May 13, 2024) — [Golf.com](https://golf.com/lifestyle/skins-app-money-games-breeze/); [Skins App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/skins-app/id6447497626)
- Press Golf app: tracks Wolf, Nassau, Skins "and every classic golf game" and "settle up from your phone" via PayPal or Venmo with pre-filled amounts — [Press Golf](https://pressgolfapp.com/)
- Beezer Golf lists Skins, Wolf, Nassau, Vegas, Scotch, Sixes among its games — [Beezer Golf](https://beezergolf.com/golf-betting-games)
- Unknown Golf: "create golf tournament side games for any style of play ... with integrated Venmo settlement" — [Unknown Golf](https://www.unknowngolf.com/golf-wager-app)
- LoopGolf, a "golf-specific cash app," launched in 30+ states bringing wagers and payment under one umbrella (Golf Digest) — [Golf Digest](https://www.golfdigest.com/story/golf-cash-app-venmo-paypal-loopgolf-betting-technology)
- TheGrint's Connect API (2025) onboarded "Calcutta (Wagering)" as a launch partner: "a real money, peer-to-peer wagering app that connects to TheGrint seamlessly," letting users "wager real money on every round played by your friends on TheGrint" with live bets on total score, birdies, doubles and props; wagers are in USDC; "Calcutta does not set lines or odds and never custody your money—it is all facilitated through smart wallets and contracts"; free to wager and to deposit/withdraw — [TheGrint: Calcutta x TheGrint](https://thegrint.com/range/post/real-money-wagers-on-live-rounds-on-thegrint); [Calcutta Golf App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/calcutta-golf/id6471930032)
- TheGrint also ships free in-app games (Medal, Stableford, Match Best Ball, Skins Best Ball, High & Low, Niner) with handicap modes and point values — [TheGrint app games](https://thegrint.com/range/post/real-money-wagers-on-live-rounds-on-thegrint)
- Venmo's US terms prohibit "transactions involving gambling, gaming and/or any other activity with an entry fee and a prize, including games of skill, unless the operator has obtained prior approval from Venmo" — [Venmo legal](https://venmo.com/legal/us-helpful-information)
- Skill-game legal framing: gambling requires prize + chance + consideration; "most states do not expressly prohibit skill games by statute," but some (e.g., Florida) prohibit wagering on skill games too — [Braslow Legal](https://braslowlegal.com/blog/2020/7/15/a-legal-guide-to-skill-gaming); [Fasthoff Law](https://fasthofflawfirm.com/blog/skill-contests-versus-sweepstakes-entry-fees)
- Sleeper's real-money layer sits outside the app's social core: "Sleeper Player Picks" (DFS pick'em) was available in only 26 states as of May 2026 and Sleeper pulled pick'em in New York when NY fantasy rules took effect; "Sleeper Team Picks" (prediction markets via Kalshi) went live early Feb 2026 after NFA approval in Jan 2026, in all 50 states + DC under CFTC jurisdiction, with per-day deposit caps ($1,500/24h) and state limits (MA $1,000/month, MD $5,000/month) — [RotoGrinders legal states](https://rotogrinders.com/fantasy/sleeper-promo-code/legal-states); [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/144962/pickem-sleeper-pulls-games-as-ny-fantasy-sports-rules-take-effect/); [FantasyLabs](https://www.fantasylabs.com/articles/sleeper-markets/); [next.io review](https://next.io/prediction-markets/sleeper-markets/)

### Inferences
- The scorekeeper-plus-P2P-settlement model works because the app has no "consideration" flowing through it; its legal exposure is minimal, but it is technically dependent on users violating Venmo's terms, which is tolerated for friend-to-friend amounts.
- Nobody has shipped a compelling points-only ("play-money") side-bet layer in golf; social golf apps instead treat competition as leaderboards/challenges (free) and leave money to adjacent apps. A tennis app can own the play-money layer (season points, bragging rights) without any regulatory burden, and leave real money off-platform.
- The crypto-rails approach (Calcutta/USDC, non-custodial) is a 2025 workaround to payment-processor bans, not a settled legal position; treat it as high-risk.

### Gaps
- I found no court cases or regulator actions against golf side-game apps; absence of enforcement is not a legal opinion.
- Calcutta's state availability and volume are not disclosed.

## Key question 3: What did Sleeper do to win friend fantasy leagues, and what is transferable?

### Takeaway
Sleeper won by making the *league chat* the product: a free-forever commissioner tool, group chat with GIFs/reactions/DMs, per-league team avatars and animated "mascots," plus a modern UI, against incumbents (ESPN/Yahoo) that treated chat as an afterthought. It raised $40M at a $400M valuation (Sept 2021) with 3M+ users, reported 10M users by Feb 2026, and monetizes via pick'em (DFS) and, since Feb 2026, Kalshi-powered prediction markets kept in separate tabs from the social core.

### Cited Findings
- Sept 17, 2021: $40M Series C led by Andreessen Horowitz (with General Catalyst, Birchmere, Klay Thompson's foundation, Kevin Durant, Baron Davis, Shane Battier), valuation $400M ("more than quadruples"); 3M+ users; leagues typically 12–14 players; users spend "20–35 minutes on the platform per gameday"; total raised $68M over 5 rounds — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/sleeper-more-than-quadruples-valuation-to-400m/); [Clay dossier](https://www.clay.com/dossier/sleeper-funding)
- Feb 6, 2026: "Sleeper has 10 million users" and launched sports prediction markets via Kalshi in time for the Super Bowl — [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/02/06/sleeper-prediction-markets-kalshi/); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-06/sports-app-sleeper-to-partner-with-kalshi-on-prediction-markets)
- Kalshi, the venue behind Sleeper Markets, was estimated at $4B annualized revenue in July 2026, up from ~$735M in Dec 2025 (Sacra) — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/kalshi/)
- Product positioning: "Run Your League Free Forever"; "millions of players draft, chat, and talk trash with their friends on Sleeper"; "react, trash talk, send GIFs, and celebrate TDs all inside their league without switching apps"; League DMs for 1:1 and sub-groups inside the league chat panel — [Sleeper fantasy football](https://sleeper.com/fantasy-football); [21 new features in 2021](https://sleeper.com/blog/sleepers-21-new-features-in-2021/)
- Avatars: users can set a different avatar per league to represent each team; mascots can be used as profile picture or team avatar — [Sleeper 2020 recap](https://sleeper.com/blog/sleepers-fantasy-football-enhancements-upgrades/)
- Mascots originally "fought" on the matchup screen (your mascot attacked the opponent's based on scoring); Sleeper removed that in 2023 "to reduce clutter on the matchup screen... For now, mascots can be used as an animated avatar" (Aug 2023) — [Sleeper on X](https://x.com/SleeperHQ/status/1690042206965444625)
- Sleeper Player Picks (pick'em) was available in 26 states as of May 2026, deliberately more conservative than PrizePicks on ambiguous states; Team Picks (Kalshi) and Player Picks are "completely different products" in separate tabs sharing one wallet — [RotoGrinders](https://rotogrinders.com/fantasy/sleeper-promo-code/legal-states); [next.io](https://next.io/prediction-markets/sleeper-markets/)
- Sleeper publishes a public read-only API (leagues, rosters, matchups) — [Sleeper API docs](https://docs.sleeper.com/)
- Estimates of Sleeper revenue vary wildly ($100K–$5M vs $32.2M/yr in aggregator profiles); no disclosed figure — [Growjo](https://growjo.com/company/Sleeper); [Owler](https://www.owler.com/company/sleeper1)

### Inferences
- The transferable Sleeper playbook: (1) free forever for the group organizer; (2) chat is first-class, not a tab; (3) identity per group (team avatar per league) so the same person can be a different character in each friend circle; (4) playful collectible identity (mascots) that survived even when the gimmick (mascot fights) was cut; (5) money products added *later*, in separate tabs, after the social graph was locked in.
- Sleeper's "mascot fight" removal is a useful warning: gamified animation on the core screen was judged clutter; collectible avatars stayed because they are identity, not noise.
- Session length ("20–35 min per gameday") came from live scoring + chat during the event, which maps to live match scoring + court-side chat in tennis.

### Gaps
- Sleeper's 2025–2026 revenue, paid conversion and DAU/MAU are undisclosed. The "7M active users in early 2025" figure appears only on low-quality aggregator blogs; the 10M figure (Feb 2026) comes from trade press and Bloomberg.

## Key question 4: Hooper / basketball run-finder and run-club apps — did "who's at the court right now" ever work?

### Takeaway
Live court presence has repeatedly spiked on virality and then stalled on density: Hoop Maps (2017) went from ~1,000 to 21,000+ users in weeks after ESPN coverage, but no mature business emerged; Fullcourt still offers "Live Court Traffic" over a 60,000-court database but its last iOS update was Sept 2024 with only 185 ratings; the brand "Hooper" today is an AI stats/highlights app, not a run finder. By contrast, run clubs on Strava (scheduled, club-based, not "who's there now") grew 3.5x in 2025 to 1M+ clubs.

### Cited Findings
- Hoop Maps (Sacramento, twins Donte and Dominic Morris): map/list of pickup games with blue (about to start) and orange (in progress) icons; users create games with time/place/duration; Facebook friend connect and in-app messaging; "added 10,000 users in the past week alone" (TechCrunch, Mar 22, 2017); grew from ~1,000 to 21,000+ users in weeks after March Madness and ESPN coverage — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/03/22/no-sweat-hoop-maps-makes-finding-a-game-of-pickup-basketball-as-easy-as-a-tap); [State Hornet](https://statehornet.com/2017/04/twin-alumni-gain-national-attention-for-hoop-maps-app)
- Fullcourt (iPlayHoops LLC): "Live Court Traffic: see who's hooping right now," 60,000+ court locations, real-time check-ins, feed/messaging; free with a $4.99 IAP; v4.3.2 last updated Sept 11, 2024; 4.4 stars on 185 ratings; reviewers note "not every area has as much info added" — [Fullcourt App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fullcourt-pickup-basketball/id1083156787)
- Other live-presence apps still listed: GOATS ("see who's playing before you head out... real-time check-ins"), ATH ("Are They Hooping": view gym activity, check in), Pick-N-Play (auto check-in on arrival), Ballnow, Ball Up, HoopRun, Pickup (2025), Hoop Find (2026) — [GOATS](https://www.goatssportsapp.com/); [ATH](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ath-pickup-basketball-app/id1308216985); [Pick-N-Play](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pick-n-play/id1448919636); [Pickup](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pickup-basketball-runs/id6743771131); [Hoop Find](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hoop-find/id6760759943)
- "Hooper" (hooper.gg, App Store id 6458646707) is an AI app that generates stats and highlight clips from a phone recording of a game, not a run finder — [Hooper](https://www.hooper.gg/); [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/hooper-2)
- Strava Year in Sport 2025 (Dec 2025): new clubs nearly quadrupled, 1M total clubs, running clubs +3.5x, hiking clubs +5.8x, club-organized events +1.5x YoY; 180M users; Brazil ~800% increase in new clubs; 66% of runners/cyclists hit a segment PR YTD (80% of Boomers vs 56% of Gen Z) — [Strava press](https://press.strava.com/articles/strava-releases-12th-annual-year-in-sport-trend-report-2025); [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/strava-mid-year-data-running-cycling/); [Running Lookout, May 9 2026](https://runninglookout.com/news/strava-2026-community-report-running-clubs-3-5x-growth-gen-z/)
- Strava acquired Runna (UK training-plan app, ~150 staff, launched 2022) on Apr 17, 2025; apps kept separate; terms undisclosed — [Running Industry Alliance](https://runningindustryalliance.com/?p=22344)

### Inferences
- "Who's there now" is a two-sided density problem at the single-venue level; it only works where one venue has enough regulars to keep the signal fresh (gyms, a few flagship parks). Every surviving basketball app has drifted toward scheduled runs (host-created games) or gym partnerships rather than passive check-ins.
- Strava's winning version of "venue presence" is asynchronous and historical (segments: who has run this, and how many times — Local Legends), plus scheduled club events, which never go stale the way a live check-in does.
- For tennis, a court's *history* (who played here, last played, win/loss on this court) is the durable version of presence; live "someone's here" should be opt-in and secondary.

### Gaps
- I found no post-mortem explaining Hoop Maps' outcome (Crunchbase lists it, but no shutdown announcement). Its App Store status is unverified.
- No app named "Ballers" with a court-presence feature surfaced in searches; it may be defunct or regional.

## Key question 5: Course/venue data — how golf apps source course maps, and the tennis equivalent

### Takeaway
Golf course maps are a licensed asset: iGolf (40,000+ courses) licenses polygons/scorecards to apps and devices; Shot Scope, SkyCaddie and Golf Intelligence map in-house (walking courses or daily satellite updates); only the largest apps have in-house databases. Tennis has no equivalent vendor, but it has far richer open data: OpenStreetMap already contains 500,000+ tennis court polygons under ODbL, city GIS portals publish court inventories, and Pickleheads (USA Pickleball's official finder) shows a crowdsourced venue database can reach 82,000+ courts.

### Cited Findings
- iGolf: "world's largest and most accurate GPS-enabled golf course database covering over 40,000 golf courses," licensing tee-box and scorecard data to mobile apps and GPS devices; internal mapping staff validate every file — [iGolf course data](https://igolf.com/solutions/golf-course-data/); [iGolf mobile apps](https://igolf.com/industries/mobile-apps/)
- Shot Scope: "Unlike many other GPS devices that buy their course mapping from third parties, Shot Scope owns and manages all golf course GPS mapping," 42,000+ courses — [Shot Scope](https://shotscope.com/us/why-shot-scope/superior-course-mapping/)
- SkyCaddie sends professional mappers to walk new and updated courses — [SkyGolf methodology](https://skygolf.com/pages/mapping-methodology)
- Golf Intelligence claims twenty years of proprietary data with daily updates since 2007; Maps4Golf sells graphics + GPS course data by plan — [Golf Intelligence](https://golfintelligence.com/golf-course-database/); [Maps4Golf](https://maps4golf.com/plans-and-pricing/)
- Course counts claimed by apps: Golfshot 47,000+, Hole19 42,000+, Shot Scope 42,000+ — see sources above
- OpenTennisMap catalogs "over 500,000 tennis courts globally" from OpenStreetMap, with corrections routed through OSM Notes — [OpenTennisMap](https://opentennismap.org/)
- An open-source single-file map (jeremydo/sports-courts-map) pulls surface, lighting and access from OSM; Court Map USA compiles courts from OSM (ODbL) plus player contributions — [GitHub](https://github.com/jeremydo/sports-courts-map); [Court Map USA](https://courtmapusa.com/)
- City/state GIS inventories exist: City of Phoenix "Tennis Courts and Pickleball" open dataset (ArcGIS REST layer) and Colorado Tennis Courts geodata — [Phoenix Open Data](https://phoenixopendata.com/dataset/tennis-courts-and-pickleball); [Colorado geodata](https://geodata.colorado.gov/datasets/colorado-tennis-courts)
- Pickleheads, USA Pickleball's official court/game finder, added 2,300+ locations in 2025 to reach 18,258 locations; its database holds 82,613 courts with 14,155 added in 2024 — [USA Pickleball growth report](https://usapickleball.org/about/annual-growth-report/); [Pickleheads statistics](https://www.pickleheads.com/guides/pickleball-statistics)
- OSM tag `sport=pickleball` is documented, with tennis courts conventionally `leisure=pitch` + `sport=tennis` — [OSM wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Tag:sport%3Dpickleball)

### Inferences
- Golf apps had to buy or build maps because yardage accuracy is the product; a tennis app's "court as level" needs only a stable venue ID, geometry, surface, lighting, court count and booking/fee rules, all of which OSM plus city datasets already provide for free. The defensible asset is therefore not the map but the *history layered on the venue* (matches played there, regulars, conditions), as Strava did with segments.
- ODbL share-alike applies to derived *databases* of OSM data; keeping match history in a separate database keyed to OSM IDs avoids contaminating proprietary data.

### Gaps
- I did not find license pricing for iGolf or Maps4Golf.
- USTA has no public court-inventory API that I could find; Pickleheads' data is proprietary (not open), so a tennis app could not reuse it without a deal.

## Key question 6: What killed the golf/social sports apps that died, and what consolidated?

### Takeaway
Pure "social network for golfers" apps (GolfMatch) faded without press; hardware-dependent apps died with their hardware (Zepp Golf after losing a patent case to Blast, 2023). The survivors consolidated into two stacks: course-operations (Lightspeed bought Chronogolf for ~$10M in 2019 and now serves 2,000+ courses) and competition/handicap/GPS (Golf Genius rolled up golfscoring.net 2020, SwingU and Golfshot/Shotzoom in 2024, now serving ~11,000 facilities). Arccos stayed independent by taking PGA Tour and OEM money ($20M Series C, Aug 2023). Topgolf was spun off to PE at a $1.1B valuation in Nov 2025, roughly half its 2020 merger value.

### Cited Findings
- Lightspeed acquired Chronogolf (Montreal) May 2019; PitchBook lists the deal at $10.1M; Chronogolf was rebranded Lightspeed Golf; Lightspeed says it "works with over 2,000 of the world's best golf courses" — [Hospitality Tech](https://hospitalitytech.com/lightspeed-pos-buys-chronogolf); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/65055-61); [Lightspeed Golf](https://lightspeedhq.com/golf)
- Golf Genius acquisitions: golfscoring.net (Feb 14, 2020, 300+ leagues), Tournament Expert (Mar 2020), Impact Tournament Systems (Jun 2020), ProTeam Golf (Jul 2020), Event-Man (Dec 2020), Twilight Golf Association (Nov 2021), Operation 36 (Jan 2022), Shotzoom/Golfshot + CoachNow (Feb 13, 2024; Golfshot 8M+ registered users, 45%+ outside the US), Swing by Swing/SwingU (Jun 4, 2024). ### Inferences
- Consumer golf apps have not produced a venture-scale outcome on their own; the exits were strategic tuck-ins into B2B platforms (Golf Genius, Lightspeed) that already monetize courses and events. The B2C app became a distribution channel for B2B software and vice versa.
- What died: apps whose value was tied to a hardware device (Zepp), and social-network-first apps without a utility hook (GolfMatch). What lived: apps with a daily-use utility (GPS/scorecard/handicap) that added social on top.
- Zwift's trajectory (price hike, free competitor, layoffs) is the cautionary case for subscription-only social sports platforms with no venue or real-world anchor.

## Other amateur-sport social apps with "venue as level" or friend-league mechanics (brief)

### Takeaway
The venue-as-level pattern already exists and works when it is historical and asynchronous (Strava segments/Local Legends, Toptracer range leaderboards, TheGrint King of the Course, GHIN Challenges); it fails when it depends on live presence (basketball check-ins). Friend-league mechanics work when the organizer tool is free and chat is core (Sleeper) or when a governing body supplies the rating (FargoRate, Playtomic levels).

### Cited Findings
- Strava Local Legends launched June 10, 2020 (7 US states), worldwide July 15, 2020: the athlete who completes a segment the most times in a rolling 90-day window gets the laurel; it rewards consistency rather than speed and was free, while the same month Strava moved full segment leaderboards (KOM/QOM) and route planning behind the paywall — [DC Rainmaker](https://dcrainmaker.com/2020/06/strava-legends-feature.html); [BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/strava-local-legend/); [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2020/06/10/new-strava-local-legends-segments-captain-but-not-as-we-know-them)
- Strava 2025: 14B kudos (+27%), 1M+ clubs, club events +1.5x; Gen Z (16–26) majority plan to use Strava more and Instagram/TikTok the same or less in 2026 (May 9, 2026) — [Running Lookout](https://runninglookout.com/news/strava-2026-community-report-running-clubs-3-5x-growth-gen-z/)
- Playtomic level: 0–7 scale in 0.25 steps; one-time self-assessment sets the start; it then moves only after *competitive* matches based on result, opponent levels, partner level and a "reliability" score that rises with match count; beating higher-level players moves you more — [Playtomic help: level system](https://playerhelp.playtomic.com/hc/en-gb/articles/43310980754193-How-the-Playtomic-level-system-works); [Playtomic help: ups & downs](https://help.playtomic.com/en/articles/3112292-the-playtomic-levels-algorithm-ups-downs)
- Toptracer Range app: shot history by club, local and global leaderboards per game mode, mirrored on range TVs; 750+ ranges, 32 countries — [Toptracer App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1336223555)
- Bowling: LeagueSecretary.com app has 100K+ Google Play downloads, 4.6 stars from 1.19K reviews; standings/history are free, "Cloud Access" is an optional annual subscription that removes ads — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.leaguesecretary.mobile&hl=en_US); [LeagueSecretary services](https://www.leaguesecretary.com/misc/services)
- Darts: DartConnect league pricing starts at $6 per player per singles season, paid by the league so individuals need no premium membership — [DartConnect league pricing](https://www.dartconnect.com/leagues/league-pricing/)
- Pool: FargoRate LMS is "the only league management system in the world with FargoRate built in"; the BCA Pool League scoring app is free to leagues on FargoRate LMS — [FargoRate LMS](https://fargorate.com/LMS); [CSI](https://www.playcsipool.com/csinews/bca-pool-league-scoring-app)
- Golf GameBook: user-created challenges among friends on "most birdies, best scores, most rounds" — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/golf-gamebook-scorecard-gps/id409307935)
- USGA GHIN Challenges (Oct 2026): AGA-created net leaderboards at daily-fee/municipal courses; 36 AGAs, 750+ challenges, 30,000+ golfers — [First Call Golf](https://www.firstcallgolf.com/industry-news/release/2026-10-05/usga-unveils-upgraded-ghin-mobile-app-offers-easier-to-use-interface-and-innovative-features)

### Inferences
- League tools in bowling/darts/pool are B2B2C: the league pays, players use it free, and the rating (FargoRate, USBC average) is the lock-in. Tennis has UTR and USTA NTRP as external ratings; a social app can import or approximate them but should not try to be the rating authority on day one.
- Playtomic's "only competitive matches move your level" plus a reliability score is the best-documented amateur-rating design for racket sports and is directly reusable for tennis.

### Gaps
- Sorare: not researched in depth (scope prioritization); no findings on friend-league mechanics there.
- No disclosed engagement data for Toptracer leaderboards, LeagueSecretary, DartConnect or FargoRate.

## What maps to a social tennis app

### Transferable mechanics (with the evidence behind each)
1. **Court = segment/level with history, not live presence.** Strava's segments and Local Legends (most completions in 90 days), TheGrint's King of the Course (weekly per-course leaderboard), Toptracer's per-range leaderboards and USGA's GHIN Challenges (per-course net leaderboards, 30,000 golfers in year one) all show venue-anchored, asynchronous leaderboards retain users. Build "Local Legend of this court" (most matches played there), court win/loss record, first-played/last-played, and a per-court season ladder.
2. **Free utility hook first, social on top.** Every surviving golf app made GPS + scorecard free and kept leaderboards/challenges free; paid tiers sold personal-improvement analytics. For tennis: free match scoring + court finder + friend roster + season leaderboard; charge (if at all) for personal stats, history exports, and advanced analytics, at a ~$30–50/yr value tier or ~$80–100/yr full tier (golf's price anchors as of 2026).
3. **Friend roster with per-group identity.** Sleeper lets one user carry a different avatar per league and collect animated mascots. Let players set a different avatar/nickname per friend group or per court; keep avatars as identity, not as on-screen animation (Sleeper cut mascot fights in 2023 as clutter).
4. **Chat as the core, not a tab.** Sleeper's 20–35 min gameday sessions came from live scoring + league chat with GIFs/reactions/DMs. A match-day thread per court/session with reactions and photo "Scorephotos" (TheGrint) is the equivalent.
5. **Season replay / year-in-review.** 18Birdies' free end-of-year summary (top courts, rounds, best scores) and TheGrint's Season Replay are retention and share mechanics; a "Season on this court" card for tennis is cheap and shareable.
6. **Friend-created challenges.** Golf GameBook's user-defined challenges (most birdies, most rounds) map to "most sets won," "most courts played," "most different opponents."
7. **A level that only moves on competitive matches, with a reliability score.** Playtomic's 0–7 level with reliability is the tested design; it avoids the "casual hit ruined my rating" complaint and is compatible with later UTR/NTRP import.
8. **Play-money, not real money.** Golf solved side bets as scorekeeper + Venmo settlement, and real-money layers (LoopGolf, Calcutta/USDC, Sleeper Picks) carry state-by-state restrictions and payment-processor bans. A points-only "side bet" ledger among friends (skins-style per game or set) gives the trash-talk value with zero regulatory load; leave real money off-platform.
9. **Venue data from open sources.** Use OpenStreetMap court polygons (500,000+ tennis courts via OpenTennisMap) plus city GIS layers (Phoenix, Colorado) for the base, and keep match history in a separate database keyed to OSM IDs so ODbL share-alike does not reach proprietary data. Crowdsourced corrections (Pickleheads/Court Map USA model) fill gaps.
10. **Scheduled sessions beat passive check-ins.** Strava's 1M clubs and 1.5x growth in club events, and the drift of basketball apps toward host-created runs, say "post a session at Court X at 6 pm" is the feature, with optional "I'm here" as a secondary signal.

### Mechanics that failed or carry risk
- **Live "who's at the court right now."** Hoop Maps spiked to 21,000 users in 2017 and left no trace; Fullcourt's live court traffic is still shipping but on a 2024 build with 185 ratings and reviewer complaints of empty data. Density per venue is the constraint; do not make it the headline feature.
- **Social-network-first without utility.** GolfMatch (2015–2017) pivoted to an Instagram-for-golf feed and disappeared from coverage. A feed without scoring/booking utility has no reason to be opened.
- **Hardware-dependent experiences.** Zepp Golf died with its sensor (2023). Arccos survives on ~$200/yr but needed $20M from the PGA Tour and OEMs in 2023; do not tie tennis MVP to a sensor.
- **Subscription-only with no real-world anchor and a price hike.** Zwift raised prices 33% in May 2024, lost concurrent users, laid off staff twice and bought a competitor; a free rival (MyWhoosh) took the UCI championship. Keep the free tier generous.
- **Real-money side games inside the app.** Venmo bans gambling settlement; pick'em is legal in only 26 states for Sleeper as of May 2026; crypto rails (Calcutta) are untested. Avoid.
- **Being the rating authority.** Bowling/darts/pool apps win by embedding the governing body's rating (FargoRate, USBC); golf apps defer to GHIN/WHS. Approximate a level internally (Playtomic style) but expect to integrate UTR/USTA later rather than compete with them.
- **Gamified animation on core screens.** Sleeper removed mascot battles from the matchup screen as clutter; keep gamification in profiles, trophies and season cards, not on the live score.
